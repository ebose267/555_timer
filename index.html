§1 NE555 Internal Design

The NE555 is an 8-pin mixed-signal IC introduced by Signetics in 1972. It integrates two voltage comparators, an SR flip-flop, a discharge transistor, and a resistive voltage divider — all in a single package. Understanding its internals is what separates an engineer from someone who just copies circuits.

Internal Architecture

5K–5K–5K Divider
Three equal 5 kΩ resistors in series from VCC to GND. Sets reference voltages: 2/3 VCC and 1/3 VCC. This is where the "555" name comes from.
Comparator 1 (Upper)
Non-inverting input tied to 2/3 VCC. Inverting input is Pin 6 (THRESHOLD). Fires when Pin 6 > 2/3 VCC → RESETS flip-flop → Output LOW.
Comparator 2 (Lower)
Inverting input tied to 1/3 VCC. Non-inverting input is Pin 2 (TRIGGER). Fires when Pin 2 < 1/3 VCC → SETS flip-flop → Output HIGH.
SR Flip-Flop
Bistable latch driven by the two comparators. Its Q-bar output drives the output stage and the discharge transistor.
Discharge Transistor
NPN BJT with collector at Pin 7 (DISCHARGE). When saturated (output LOW), it shorts Pin 7 to GND, rapidly discharging the timing capacitor.
Output Stage
Totem-pole output at Pin 3. Can source and sink up to 200 mA. Output is inverted relative to Q-bar of the flip-flop.

Pin Configuration

PinNameFunction
1GNDGround reference. All voltages measured relative to this.
2TRIGGERInverting input of Comparator 2. Pulling below 1/3 VCC sets the output HIGH. Active LOW.
3OUTPUTMain output. Swings between ~0V and VCC−1.7V. Can source/sink 200 mA.
4RESETActive LOW override. Pulling to GND forces output LOW regardless of other inputs. Tie to VCC if unused.
5CONTROLDirect access to 2/3 VCC reference. Used to modulate threshold externally. Decouple with 10 nF to GND if unused.

7DISCHARGECollector of internal NPN transistor. Conducts (shorts to GND) when output is LOW, discharging the timing cap.
8VCCSupply voltage: 4.5V – 16V (NE555); 2V – 16V (CMOS variants like TLC555).
⚠ DATASHEET HABIT
Always check Pin 5 (CONTROL). In most lab circuits it floats, but in noise-heavy environments (near rectifiers) it picks up ripple and shifts the threshold reference. Add a 10 nF decoupling cap from Pin 5 to GND — this one line appears in every professional 555 design.

§2 Application Calculations

APPLICATION 1 — Thermal Management (PWM Fan Control) ASTABLE MODE

The 555 in astable mode generates a continuous square wave. To achieve independent control of the ON-time (tH) and OFF-time (tL) — and thus full duty cycle control — a steering diode is added across RA, bypassing it during the capacitor charging phase.

Modified Astable Formulas (with Steering Diode)

Core Timing Equations
tH = 0.693 × RA × C   (charge time — HIGH period)
tL = 0.693 × RB × C   (discharge time — LOW period)
T = tH + tL = 0.693 × (RA + RB) × C
f = 1 / T = 1.44 / [(RA + RB) × C]
D% = (tH / T) × 100 = [RA / (RA + RB)] × 100
Note: Without the steering diode, charge goes through RA+RB giving D = (RA+RB)/(RA+2RB) — always >50%.

Worked Example — 1 kHz PWM, 70% Duty Cycle

Design target: Control fan at 70% full speed. Frequency = 1 kHz to prevent audible switching noise.

01

Choose C: Select C = 100 nF (standard ceramic, widely available)

02

Find T: T = 1/f = 1/1000 = 1 ms

03

Calculate tH and tL:

tH = D × T = 0.70 × 1ms = 0.70 ms

tL = (1 − D) × T = 0.30 × 1ms = 0.30 ms

04

Solve for RA:

RA = tH / (0.693 × C) = 0.70×10⁻³ / (0.693 × 100×10⁻⁹)

RA = 0.70×10⁻³ / 69.3×10⁻⁹ = 10.1 kΩ → use 10 kΩ

05

Solve for RB:

RB = tL / (0.693 × C) = 0.30×10⁻³ / 69.3×10⁻⁹ = 4.33 kΩ → use 4.7 kΩ

06

Verify with chosen standard values (10 kΩ, 4.7 kΩ, 100 nF):

D = 10k / (10k + 4.7k) = 10 / 14.7 = 68% ≈ 70% ✓

f = 1.44 / [(10k + 4.7k) × 100n] = 1.44 / 1.47×10⁻³ = 979 Hz ≈ 1 kHz ✓

STEERING DIODE
Place a 1N4148 fast-switching diode with its anode at Pin 7 and cathode at the junction of RA and RB. During charging, current flows VCC → RA → D → C (bypasses RB). During discharge, Pin 7 pulls down through RB only (diode is reverse-biased). This is what gives independent tH/tL control.
APPLICATION 2 — Power-Good Delay (Soft Start System) MONOSTABLE MODE

In monostable (one-shot) mode, the 555 produces a single output pulse of precise duration when triggered. On power-up, the circuit counts a fixed delay before enabling the relay that connects the load. This protects downstream devices from inrush current spikes and regulator settling transients.

Monostable Formula

Timing Equation
T = 1.1 × R × C
Where T = pulse duration (seconds), R = timing resistor (Ω), C = timing capacitor (F)
Derivation: Capacitor charges from 0V to 2/3 VCC through R. For an RC circuit: t = −RC·ln(1 − 2/3) = RC·ln(3) ≈ 1.0986·RC ≈ 1.1·RC

Worked Example — 3 Second Delay

Target: T = 3 seconds. Sufficient for rectifier capacitors to charge and regulators to stabilize.

01

Choose C: Select C = 100 µF (electrolytic — large value needed for long delays)

Note polarity — the positive terminal connects toward Pin 6/2 and pin 7.

02

Rearrange formula to find R:

R = T / (1.1 × C) = 3 / (1.1 × 100×10⁻⁶)

R = 3 / (1.1 × 10⁻⁴) = 3 / 0.00011 = 27,272 Ω → use 27 kΩ

03

Verify with standard values (27 kΩ, 100 µF):

T = 1.1 × 27,000 × 100×10⁻⁶ = 1.1 × 2.7 = 2.97 s ≈ 3 s ✓

04

Triggering: Pin 2 (TRIGGER) is pulled to GND momentarily on power-up via a 10 µF capacitor and pull-up resistor auto-trigger network. Output on Pin 3 goes HIGH for 3 seconds, activating the relay driver (NPN transistor + relay coil). After 3s, output returns LOW, relay opens, and the delay resets.

RELAY DRIVER
Pin 3 cannot drive a relay directly beyond 200 mA. Use a 2N2222 NPN transistor: base → 1 kΩ → Pin 3; collector → relay coil → VCC; emitter → GND. Add a 1N4007 flyback diode across the relay coil to suppress back-EMF when the coil de-energises.
APPLICATION 3 — Under-Voltage Fault Alarm COMPARATOR MODE

This application directly exploits the internal comparators of the 555 — no external RC timing network is needed. A resistive voltage divider scales the unregulated DC rail voltage down to the 555's reference window. When the monitored voltage drops below a threshold, the comparator output triggers, activating a fault indicator.

Internal Reference Architecture

The internal 5K–5K–5K divider (powered from VCC = 5V regulated supply) establishes two fixed references:

Internal Voltage References
V_upper (Pin 6 Threshold) = 2/3 × VCC = 2/3 × 5V = 3.33V
V_lower (Pin 2 Trigger) = 1/3 × VCC = 1/3 × 5V = 1.67V
Comparator rule: Pin 2 drops below 1.67V → Output HIGH (FAULT state)

Worked Example — Brownout Detection at 13V

Problem: The 12V regulator has a dropout voltage of ~2V, so its input must stay above 14V to maintain regulation. We set the fault threshold at 13V to trigger before total failure. The unregulated rail is monitored. The 555 runs from the stable 5V rail.

01

Set the threshold: We want Pin 2 to reach 1.67V exactly when V_monitored = 13V.

Required divider ratio: K = V_ref / V_fault = 1.67 / 13 = 0.1285

02

Design voltage divider (R1 high-side, R2 low-side):

K = R2 / (R1 + R2) = 0.1285

Choose R2 = 10 kΩ (to limit current draw from monitored rail)

R1 = R2 × (1/K − 1) = 10k × (7.78 − 1) = 10k × 6.78 = 67.8 kΩ → use 68 kΩ

03

Verify:

V_pin2 = 13 × (10k / (68k + 10k)) = 13 × 10/78 = 13 × 0.1282 = 1.667V ✓

04

Fault behaviour:

Normal (V_rail > 13V): V_pin2 > 1.67V → Comparator 2 inactive → Output LOW → LED/buzzer OFF

Fault (V_rail drops below 13V): V_pin2 < 1.67V → Comparator 2 fires → Output HIGH → FAULT LED/buzzer ON

05

Hysteresis (optional but recommended):

To prevent oscillation near the threshold, connect a feedback resistor (≈ 1 MΩ) from Pin 3 (OUTPUT) back to Pin 2 (TRIGGER). This adds a small positive feedback that creates a Schmitt trigger effect — the alarm latches ON until the supply recovers above a slightly higher voltage, preventing chattering.

ENGINEERING PRINCIPLE
The NE555 is one IC doing the job of roughly 20 discrete components (two op-amps, an SR latch, a BJT, and three precision resistors). The lesson here is not the 555 itself — it is the habit of reading an internal block diagram and reasoning from silicon architecture. Every IC you encounter in your career has this diagram in its datasheet. Learn to read it here first.
